# Research watch for autostat, November 2026

Written on 15 November 2026. It covers work from 2023 to November 2026 that the project should know, beyond what README.md ("The Automatic Statistician family", "Forecasting against the methods of 2026", "AutoGP in Python"), doc/research/context_features.md (11 November 2026) and CHANGELOG.md already cover.

**How this was checked.** Each reference was opened on arXiv, OpenReview, PMLR, a conference page or the project's own page, unless a **Flag** says otherwise. A flag also marks a detail (venue, author list, number) that the page I opened did not show. Live leaderboards (GIFT-Eval, fev-bench) did not render in my fetcher, so rankings come from papers and vendor posts that quote them, with the date of each snapshot.

**Already known, not repeated in full.** BoxLM (Li, Fox and Goodman, ICML 2024), CAKE (Suwandi et al., NeurIPS 2025), the VLM pipeline of Lee et al. (arXiv 2509.25946), the Kernel Manifold (Islam et al., arXiv 2601.05371), Yun et al. (arXiv 2605.20249), KITT (NeurIPS 2021), gallifrey (Boettner, A&A 2025), AutoBNN, Context is Key (CiK, ICML 2025) and its prompting follow-up (Ashok et al.), CAAFE, Time-LLM, Tan et al. (NeurIPS 2024), Time-MMD, ELATE, Prophet, CausalImpact and the feature libraries. They appear below only where a new item changes what to do with them.

## Headline findings

1. **Model averaging over LLM-proposed programs is now a published NeurIPS method** (Domke, "Large Language Bayes", NeurIPS 2025). It is the same move autostat makes with exp(-BIC/2) weights, with a language model as the proposal. Together with CAKE, BoxLM and AutoStan it means "LLM proposes, Bayesian score decides" is the mainstream pattern of 2025 and 2026. autostat has the scorer and the context but not yet the proposer.
2. **No paper takes typed business context (dated events, expected cycles) into GP structure search.** The gap named in the 11 November review is still open. Dr-CiK (2026) shows that retrieved context often makes forecasts worse, so a context ablation is mandatory for any claim.
3. **Foundation models moved fast.** Chronos-2 (October 2025) led GIFT-Eval and fev-bench in early 2026. Toto 2.0 (May 2026) claims first place among foundation models on GIFT-Eval and on the new contamination-resistant TIME benchmark (ICML 2026). By September 2026 the top of GIFT-Eval is held by agentic systems and routers of fine-tuned foundation models. A NeurIPS 2026 workshop paper shows that no two neighbours in GIFT-Eval's top ten are statistically separable.
4. **None of the leading foundation models returns an interpretable decomposition.** Interpretability work on them is post hoc (saliency, latent steering). The niche of autostat (a formula, a written report, calibrated structure probabilities) is not taken.
5. **The 13-series benchmark is likely contaminated for foundation models** (the airline and Mauna Loa series sit in standard pretraining corpora) and too small to separate methods. TIME and fev-bench are the 2026 answers.
6. **Kernel composition has become the standard synthetic prior** for pretraining (KernelSynth in Chronos, CauKer at ICLR 2026, TempoPFN). autostat's grammar is exactly such a generator, which opens an amortised route to fast structure search.
7. **The cubic limit is not autostat's bottleneck on short series** (4.1 hours for 144 points comes from search breadth and restarts). Amortised hyperparameter inference (Bitzer et al., UAI 2023) attacks that cost. For long series, quasiseparable or state-space solvers (celerite2, tinygp) and iterative solvers (GPyTorch, computation-aware GPs) are mature.

## 1. Automatic GP and kernel structure discovery after 2023

**Domke. "Large Language Bayes." NeurIPS 2025 (Main Track). https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.14025 ; https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/0aa749f20e2a428e40db37631262dc82-Abstract-Conference.html**
An LLM turns an informal problem description into many candidate formal models in a probabilistic programming language. Inference is run on each and the results are combined by a weighted average justified as self-normalised importance sampling (weights from each model's marginal likelihood). It gives sensible predictions from data plus a plain-language description, without a hand-written model.
*For autostat:* adopt the scheme as the theory behind `structure_probabilities` and extend it to forecasts: predict with the BIC-weighted mixture of the beam; treat any LLM-proposed kernel as one more proposal inside the same weighting.

**Dürr. "AutoStan: Autonomous Bayesian Model Improvement via Predictive Feedback." arXiv 2603.27766, March 2026 (preprint, single author).**
A command-line coding agent writes Stan models, runs MCMC and keeps or reverts each change by held-out negative log predictive density plus sampler diagnostics. On five problems it finds robust likelihoods, mixtures and hierarchical pooling and matches TabPFN on one synthetic task while staying interpretable.
*For autostat:* compare against, not adopt. It is the "agent writes the model" baseline that a referee will ask about; its keep-or-revert rule on held-out NLPD is a useful second judge beside BIC.

**Suwandi, Yin, Wang, Li, Chang and Theodoridis. "Adaptive Kernel Design for Bayesian Optimization Is a Piece of CAKE with LLMs." NeurIPS 2025. https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.17998** (already in the earlier review)
LLMs act as crossover and mutation operators on a kernel grammar and candidates are ranked by BIC combined with expected improvement.
*For autostat:* the concrete operator design to copy for an LLM proposal step in `search.py`, with BIC alone as the ranking for regression.

**Li, Fox and Goodman. "Automated Statistical Model Discovery with Language Models." ICML 2024. https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.17879** (already cited in README)
The LM proposes and critiques probabilistic programs in Box's loop and roughly matches the Automatic Statistician in a kernel DSL on six series.
*For autostat:* still the reference LLM baseline; no public code, so reimplement its kernel-DSL variant on the 13 series if a comparison is wanted.

**Lee, Hyeon-Woo, Ye-Bin, Nam and Oh. "Automated Model Discovery via Multi-modal & Multi-step Pipeline." arXiv 2509.25946, 2025.** (already cited in README)
AnalyzerVLM proposes models from plots; EvaluatorVLM scores them quantitatively and visually. It compares against the Automatic Statistician, BoxLM, ARIMA and Prophet on airline and Mauna Loa.
**Flag:** the arXiv page lists no venue and no code; a search snippet suggests a NeurIPS 2025 proceedings entry that I could not confirm.
*For autostat:* it reports beating the Automatic Statistician on the same series, so rerun its datasets with the port and state the result in README.

**Islam, Padhy, Allaire and Arróyave. "The Kernel Manifold." arXiv 2601.05371, January 2026** and **Yun, Shin, Song, Lee and Park. arXiv 2605.20249, May 2026.** (both in the earlier review)
Geometric kernel embeddings for Bayesian optimisation over kernels; LLM-driven kernel evolution for high-dimensional BO. Both benchmark against an LLM-guided CAKE variant.
*For autostat:* ignore for now (BO targets, not time series); revisit only if structure embeddings become features.

**Bitzer, Meister and Zimmer. "Amortized Inference for Gaussian Process Hyperparameters of Structured Kernels." UAI 2023, PMLR 216:184-194. https://proceedings.mlr.press/v216/bitzer23a.html**
A transformer conditioned on both the dataset and a symbolic kernel structure returns hyperparameters for any kernel in a grammar family, with no retraining per structure. Inference time drops by orders of magnitude at competitive test performance and it allows fast ensembling over structures.
*For autostat:* adopt as the main lever on search cost. Use its output as the initial point for the existing optimiser, so BIC is still computed on fitted parameters; train on samples from autostat's own grammar.

**Sharma, Tiwari, Nema and Pareek. "Mechanistic Evidence for Spectral Structures in Prior-Data Fitted Networks." arXiv 2601.21731 (v4, October 2026; first posted as "Amortized Spectral Kernel Discovery via Prior-Data Fitted Network").**
Linear probes recover the context's frequency from seven PFNs with R² at least 0.95; a Filter Bank Decoder turns a frozen PFN representation into an explicit stationary kernel via Bochner's theorem, usable for GP regression at about 200 times lower cost than deep kernel learning.
*For autostat:* watch. A PFN-decoded spectrum could seed period hints when the business context gives none; not mature enough to adopt.

**Becker, Huot, Mansinghka and others. "Probabilistic Programming with Vectorized Programmable Inference" (GenJAX). POPL 2026, PACMPL, DOI 10.1145/3776729. https://popl26.sigplan.org/details/POPL-2026-popl-research-papers/89/Probabilistic-Programming-with-Vectorized-Programmable-Inference ; https://genjax.gen.dev/**
GenJAX is Gen's successor on JAX: vectorisation of probabilistic programs is a source-to-source transformation on a core calculus, giving performance close to hand-written JAX for SMC and MCMC patterns. Artifact v1.0.13 on Zenodo (November 2025).
**Flag:** full author list not read.
*For autostat:* note in README that the probsys line has moved from Gen.jl to GenJAX; a GenJAX AutoGP would be the natural next reference implementation to test `autostat.smc` against. No sign yet that AutoGP itself has been ported (gallifrey remains the JAX port).

**Saad, Mansinghka et al. after ICML 2023.** I searched for a 2024 to 2026 AutoGP follow-up by the original authors and found none beyond AutoGP.jl releases. **Flag:** absence of evidence, not evidence of absence; check the probsys GitHub organisation before the next release.

**Xie, Feofanov, Odonnat, Zan, Alonso, Zhang, Palpanas, Pan, Zhang and Redko. "CauKer: Classification Time Series Foundation Models Can Be Pretrained on Synthetic Data." ICLR 2026 (Oral). https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.02879**
Series are sampled from GP priors built by kernel composition, then passed through random structural causal models; pretraining on this synthetic data alone gives clean scaling laws from 10K to 10M samples and competitive zero-shot UCR accuracy.
*For autostat:* the grammar is already such a generator; expose `grammar.sample_structures()` and sampled series, which serves Bitzer-style amortisation, synthetic recovery tests and anyone pretraining on kernel priors.

**Moroshan, Siems, Zela, Carstensen and Hutter. "TempoPFN: Synthetic Pre-training of Linear RNNs for Zero-shot Time Series Forecasting." arXiv 2510.25502 (v4, February 2026).**
A 35M-parameter linear RNN (GatedDeltaProduct) pretrained only on synthetic series (SDEs, GPs, audio synthesis); it beats every other synthetic-only model on GIFT-Eval.
**Flag:** a NeurIPS 2026 poster page appeared in search; I did not open it.
*For autostat:* add as a benchmark entry: it is the closest foundation model to "a GP prior learned by a network", so it tests whether explicit structure search still pays.

## 2. Agents that explain time series in language and how they are evaluated

**Zhao, Zhang and others. "TimeSeriesScientist: A General-Purpose AI Agent for Time Series Analysis." arXiv 2510.01538, October 2025.**
Four LLM agents handle diagnostics and preprocessing, model shortlisting, forecasting with ensembling and a written report. The authors report 10.4% lower error than statistical baselines and 38.2% lower than LLM baselines on eight benchmarks.
**Flag:** no peer-reviewed venue; claims are the authors' own.
*For autostat:* the most direct "automatic statistician as LLM agent" competitor. Compare its reports with autostat's on the 13 series: autostat's claims are derived from a fitted model, theirs from an LLM, which is the argument to make.

**Garza and Rosillo. "TimeCopilot." arXiv 2509.00616 (v3, November 2025).**
An open-source agentic forecasting framework: an LLM orchestrates foundation, statistical and ML models through feature analysis, cross-validated model selection and forecasting, then answers questions about its reasoning. It reports state-of-the-art probabilistic accuracy on GIFT-Eval at low cost.
**Flag:** the arXiv page gives no code link (the package is on PyPI per the paper).
*For autostat:* compare against and borrow the interface idea: autostat's MCP server could expose "why this structure" queries backed by the BIC table, which an LLM agent cannot fake.

**Gwiazda, Cai, Goswami, Choudhry and Dubrawski. "TimeSeriesExamAgent: Creating Time Series Reasoning Benchmarks at Scale." ICLR 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.10291** (with the original **Cai, Choudhry, Goswami and Dubrawski. "TimeSeriesExam." arXiv 2410.14752, 2024**; **Flag:** I did not confirm the 2024 venue.)
TimeSeriesExam is a multiple-choice exam on synthetic series covering pattern recognition, noise, similarity, anomalies and causality; the agent version generates exams from real healthcare, finance and weather data. LLMs remain weak on abstract reasoning and domain tasks.
*For autostat:* use as an evaluation of the report: answer the exam questions from autostat's fitted structure (is there a trend, a period, a changepoint) and compare with LLMs. This turns "interpretable" into a score.

**Kong et al. "Time-MQA: Time Series Multi-Task Question Answering with Context Enhancement." ACL 2025 (Long). https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.01875**
Unifies forecasting, imputation, anomaly detection, classification and open-ended reasoning as question answering, with the TSQA dataset of about 200k pairs; continued pretraining of 7B and 8B LLMs improves temporal reasoning.
*For autostat:* ignore as training data; borrow its open-ended reasoning questions for the report evaluation above.

**Zhou, Yashwante, Fisher, Sampieri, Zhou, Galasso and Yu. "CaTS-Bench: Can Language Models Describe Time Series?" arXiv 2509.20823 (v6, April 2026).**
Time series captioning across 11 domains with 1,746 human-rewritten captions and 910 diagnostic multiple-choice questions with numeric metrics; leading VLMs miss numeric detail, fine-tuning on synthetic captions helps.
**Flag:** a search result lists it in Findings of ACL 2026; the arXiv page shows no venue.
*For autostat:* the closest benchmark to what autostat's reports do. Generate autostat captions for the test set and score them; numeric faithfulness is where a model-based report should win.

**Tang, Williams, Ashok, Zheng, Gupta, Sun, Drouin, Laradji, Marcotte and Zantedeschi. "Dr-CiK: A Benchmark testing Deep Research for Context-Aided Forecasting." arXiv 2605.27904 (v2, October 2026; an ICML 2026 workshop version exists).**
240 tasks where an agent must retrieve the context itself from a corpus with distractors. Agents recover little of the true evidence, cite distractors heavily and can forecast worse with retrieved context than with none.
*For autostat:* the strongest argument for keeping context typed and human-supplied (YAML `Context`) and for a "misleading context" ablation; cite it in doc/research.

**Chang, Shi, Cao, Yang, Hwang, Wang, Pang, Wang, Liu, Peng and Chen. "A Survey of Reasoning and Agentic Systems in Time Series with Large Language Models." TMLR (arXiv 2509.11575, v3 June 2026).**
Organises methods by reasoning structure (direct, chain, branching) against goals including explanation and causal inference. It lists the benchmarks (MTBench, TSQA and others). It calls for benchmarks tying reasoning quality to utility.
*For autostat:* the citation to position autostat as a "verification-first" explainer, where every sentence is backed by a fitted component.

**Xu, Tang and others. "LLM-based Agents for Forecasting and Prediction: Methods, Training, Evaluation and Applications." arXiv 2608.23058, 2026.**
Survey of forecasting agents: standalone LLM workflows, tool and retrieval agents and hybrids with statistical or foundation models.
**Flag:** seen through search excerpts only; I did not open the full text.
*For autostat:* background only.

## 3. Time-series foundation models and benchmarks, late 2026

**Aksu, Woo, Liu, Liu, Liu, Savarese, Xiong and Sahoo. "GIFT-Eval: A Benchmark For General Time Series Forecasting Model Evaluation." arXiv 2410.10393, 2024.**
23 datasets, over 144,000 series, seven domains and ten frequencies, with a non-overlapping pretraining corpus; by 2026 the leaderboard scores 97 configurations and holds about 130 entries, including an agentic category.
**Flag:** no venue on the arXiv page; the live leaderboard did not load.
*For autostat:* too large for exact GP search; use only a small, frequency-stratified subset; report against the leaderboard entries rather than submitting.

**Shchur, Ansari, Turkmen, Stella, Erickson, Guerron, Bohlke-Schneider and Wang. "fev-bench: A Realistic Benchmark for Time Series Forecasting." NeurIPS 2026 Evaluations and Datasets Track. https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.26468 (v5, 4 November 2026)**
100 tasks in seven domains, 46 with covariates, aggregated by win rate and skill score with bootstrap confidence intervals; ships the `fev` library.
**Flag:** live leaderboard did not load; third-party mirrors put Chronos-2 first in 2026.
*For autostat:* adopt `fev` for scoring and its bootstrap intervals; its covariate tasks with known future covariates are the natural test for `Context` events.

**Qiao, Pan, Wang, Zhukova, Liu, Jiang, Wen, Long, Jin and Liu. "It's TIME: Towards the Next Generation of Time Series Forecasting Benchmarks." ICML 2026, PMLR 306:101344-101377. https://proceedings.mlr.press/v306/qiao26g.html**
50 fresh datasets and 98 tasks built for strict zero-shot evaluation without pretraining leakage, with "pattern-level" evaluation by STL-based structural features rather than dataset labels; 12 foundation models evaluated.
*For autostat:* the best target for a fair comparison: fresh data removes the contamination advantage of foundation models on the 13 classic series; its pattern-level slices (trend, seasonality strength) match what autostat claims to detect.

**Sikder and Pillutla. "What a Forecasting Leaderboard Can Establish: Resolution Reporting for Zero-Shot Time-Series Models." NeurIPS 2026 workshop (Foundation Models for Temporal Systems). https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/175015**
GIFT-Eval's resolution is coarse: no adjacent pair in its top ten is reliably separated and third-party submissions reversed 20 pairs; fev-bench is stable. Shortening context to 512 steps hurt TimesFM-2.5 and Toto by more than the whole spread of the top ten.
*For autostat:* apply the same honesty to benchmark/RESULTS.md: report which methods are inseparable from the leader on 13 series; record the context length given to each foundation model.

**Ansari et al. (23 authors). "Chronos-2: From Univariate to Universal Forecasting." arXiv 2510.15821, October 2025.**
One pretrained model for univariate, multivariate and covariate-informed forecasting by in-context learning with group attention; state of the art on fev-bench, GIFT-Eval and Chronos Benchmark II at release. A Rossmann case study shows promotions and holidays as covariates turning a flat forecast into an accurate one.
*For autostat:* already benchmarked; add a run that passes the `Context` events as known future covariates, so the comparison with context-aware autostat is like for like.

**Auer et al. "TiRex: Zero-Shot Forecasting Across Long and Short Horizons with Enhanced In-Context Learning." NeurIPS 2025. https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.23719**
An xLSTM model (35M parameters) with contiguous patch masking; strong on GIFT-Eval and fev-bench, statistically tied with TimesFM-2.5 in the fev-bench paper per a mirror.
**Flag:** weights under the NXAI community licence, not open source; full author list not read.
*For autostat:* add to the benchmark (small, fast); note the licence in RESULTS.md.

**Cohen et al. "This Time is Different: An Observability Perspective on Time Series Foundation Models" (Toto and BOOM). arXiv 2505.14766, 2025** and **Khwaja, Lettieri, Woo, Belouadah et al. (13 authors). "Toto 2.0: Time Series Forecasting Enters the Scaling Era." arXiv 2605.20119, May 2026.** Blog: https://datadoghq.com/blog/ai/toto-2/
Toto 2.0 releases five Apache 2.0 checkpoints from 4M to 2.5B parameters and reports predictable scaling. Datadog reports first place among foundation models on GIFT-Eval (CRPS rank 19.5 against 22.4 for Chronos-2), first on TIME and on BOOM.
**Flag:** ranks are vendor-reported (May 2026); the first author of the 2025 paper is from a search snippet.
*For autostat:* add Toto 2.0 (313M or 1B) to the benchmark; it is the open-weight leader of mid 2026.

**Salesforce AI Research. "Moirai 2.0: When Less Is More for Time Series Forecasting." arXiv 2511.11698 (v3, February 2026).**
A decoder-only quantile model trained on 36M series, thirty times smaller and twice as fast as Moirai 1.0-Large, near the top of GIFT-Eval among pretrained models; no cross-variate modelling.
**Flag:** author list not read.
*For autostat:* the task brief mentions Moirai but the README list does not; add Moirai 2.0 small, it is cheap.

**Thierry and Rochet. "TW3Cast: A Frozen Router of Lightly Fine-Tuned Foundation Models for Time-Series Forecasting on GIFT-Eval, Selected Entirely on the Training Split." arXiv 2609.28506, September 2026.**
Third of 130 GIFT-Eval entries by mean MASE rank (14 September 2026) with no LLM at runtime: a routing table chooses per configuration between fine-tuned Chronos-2, TiRex or Toto specialists and blends. The two entries above it are agentic systems.
*For autostat:* the state of the art in late 2026 is routing and ensembling of foundation models, not one model. A fair "2026" row in RESULTS.md is a simple per-series selection among foundation models by rolling-origin validation, which the skrub and skore tooling already supports.

**Interpretable decompositions from foundation models.** TEMPO (Cao et al., ICLR 2024, https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.04948) feeds STL components to a pretrained transformer; a 2025 saliency study (arXiv 2505.13100) finds that TimesFM underweights trend. **Flag:** I read these through search abstracts only. None of Chronos-2, TimesFM 2.5, Toto 2.0, TiRex or Moirai 2.0 outputs a decomposition or formula.
*For autostat:* keep the interpretability claim; it is still unoccupied by the accuracy leaders.

## 4. Decomposition, changepoints and event effects, 2024 to 2026

**Thumm and Chen. "Interventional Time Series Priors for Causal Foundation Models." ICLR 2026 TSALM workshop. https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.11090**
CausalTimePrior generates synthetic temporal structural causal models with paired observational and interventional series (hard, soft and time-varying interventions, regime switches). PFNs trained on it estimate causal effects in context on held-out models.
*For autostat:* use such paired series as ground truth to validate `event_effect` (true lift known), rather than only the synthetic jump family.

**DoTime: A Synthetic Benchmark Generator for Interventional and Counterfactual Time Series. arXiv 2607.27263, 2026.**
Regime-switching structural causal models presented as a generalisation of interrupted time series.
**Flag:** seen only as a search abstract; authors not read.
*For autostat:* same use as CausalTimePrior; choose whichever has usable code.

**Tveten, Kolstø and Moen. "skchange: Fast and Flexible Algorithms for Changepoint Detection." arXiv 2608.19767, August 2026.**
A scikit-learn-style, Numba-accelerated library of changepoint and anomalous-segment detectors (cost-based and test-based) with data-driven penalty calibration.
*For autostat:* compare against: on series with known event dates, report whether autostat's `CP` locations agree with skchange's (PELT-type) detections; it is also a cheap pre-screen to propose candidate dates when the context has none.

**PyMC Labs. CausalPy (software, Apache 2.0). https://github.com/pymc-labs/CausalPy**
Bayesian quasi-experiments in PyMC: interrupted time series, synthetic control, difference in differences and others.
**Flag:** no paper and no release date shown on the page.
*For autostat:* the comparator for `event_effect`: same dated event, CausalPy ITS interval against autostat's counterfactual interval.

**Zhao et al. BEAST (Rbeast and Pybeast). Remote Sensing of Environment 2019 (before the window). https://github.com/zhaokg/Pybeast**
Bayesian model averaging over trend and season decompositions with changepoint probabilities.
**Flag:** journal details from memory of the method, not reopened.
*For autostat:* the closest older method that also outputs P(changepoint at t); worth one comparison line in the features evaluation.

**Foundation models as counterfactual forecasters.** I found no peer-reviewed study that validates Chronos or TimesFM forecasts as counterfactuals for interrupted time series. *For autostat:* a small experiment (pre-event forecast by Chronos-2 against autostat's counterfactual on CausalTimePrior data) would be novel and cheap.

## 5. Scalable GP inference to lift the cubic limit

**Wenger, Wu, Hennig, Gardner, Pleiss and Cunningham. "Computation-Aware Gaussian Processes: Model Selection And Linear-Time Inference." NeurIPS 2024. https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.01036**
A training loss for hyperparameters under computation-aware inference with sparse actions gives linear-time model selection whose uncertainty accounts for the approximation; it beats SGPR, CGGP and SVGP and runs on 1.8M points in hours on one GPU.
*For autostat:* the principled option if BIC must be computed on long series: its loss is a proper evidence-like objective, unlike plain inducing-point bounds which bias BIC comparisons between structures.

**Lin, Padhy, Antorán, Tripp, Terenin, Szepesvári, Hernández-Lobato and Janz. "Stochastic Gradient Descent for Gaussian Processes Done Right." ICLR 2024. https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.20581**
Stochastic dual descent solves the GP linear system faster than preconditioned CG and variational GPs on UCI and BO tasks.
*For autostat:* useful for the posterior mean in reports on long series; does not give the log determinant needed by BIC, so not a full backend.

**Riutort-Mayol, Bürkner, Andersen, Solin and Vehtari. "Practical Hilbert space approximate Bayesian Gaussian processes for probabilistic programming." Statistics and Computing 33, 17, 2023. https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.11408**
Basis-function approximations of stationary kernels (and a periodic expansion) with rules for basis size and boundary; implemented in PyMC (`HSGP`, `HSGPPeriodic`) and NumPyro.
**Flag:** journal page not opened; reference taken from PyMC and NumPyro docs.
*For autostat:* the simplest route to O(n m²) for SE, Matérn and Per components; changepoint operators multiply by sigmoids, which keep a low-rank form, so a basis backend is feasible for the 2014 grammar.

**Foreman-Mackey et al. celerite2 and tinygp `quasisep`. https://tinygp.readthedocs.io/en/stable/tutorials/quasisep.html**
O(n) GPs on sorted 1-D inputs for quasiseparable kernels (Matérn-3/2, SHO, celerite terms) with sums and products allowed; tinygp warns that cost grows quickly with each added or multiplied term.
**Flag:** celerite2 papers not reopened in this watch.
*For autostat:* the practical backend for long series with Matérn and quasi-periodic terms; SE and exact Per need approximation, so expect small BIC shifts and test them against the exact path.

**Ding, Tuo and Zhou. "Beyond State Space Representation: A General Theory for Kernel Packets." arXiv 2402.04022 (v5, November 2025); and Chen, Ding and Tuo, JMLR 2022 (arXiv 2203.03116).**
Compactly supported combinations of Matérn covariances give exact GP inference in linear time for half-integer Matérn in 1-D, extended to scattered multi-dimensional inputs.
**Flag:** the 2024 paper shows no venue.
*For autostat:* watch; exact (not approximate) linear-time Matérn is attractive for the test-suite comparison against the cubic path, but SE and Per are not covered.

**GPyTorch and linear_operator status.** GPyTorch 1.15.2 (28 February 2026) with linear_operator 0.6.1; active maintenance, no deprecation. https://pypi.org/project/gpytorch/
*For autostat:* since autostat is already in torch, GPyTorch's CG and Lanczos log-determinant estimators (through linear_operator) are the lowest-effort iterative backend for n between about 1,000 and 50,000.

**GPJax (Pinder and Dodd, JOSS 2022, https://joss.theoj.org/papers/10.21105/joss.04455)** and **smolgp (state-space GPs on tinygp, https://pypi.org/project/smolgp/).**
**Flag:** I could not confirm 2026 release activity for GPJax or a release date for smolgp.
*For autostat:* ignore; JAX backends would split the code base.

**Where the time goes.** README reports 4.1 hours for 144 points: the cost is the number of candidate structures times random restarts, not n. Lifting the cubic limit only matters once series longer than the 1,000-bin cap are in scope; amortised initialisation (Bitzer et al.) and BIC-weighted pruning matter first.

## Ranked actions

1. **Forecast with the BIC-weighted mixture of the beam, not only the best model.**
   Why: Large Language Bayes and AutoGP both show averaging over structures is the calibrated choice; the weights already exist in `structure_probabilities`. It should widen intervals where structures disagree and is the cheapest accuracy gain.
   Effort S to M. Touches `src/autostat/search.py`, `src/autostat/model.py`, `src/autostat/features.py`, `benchmark/run_2026.py`.

2. **Add Toto 2.0, TiRex, Moirai 2.0 and TempoPFN to the benchmark, plus a "select per series among foundation models" row.**
   Why: these are the open-weight leaders of 2026 and late-2026 leaderboards are led by routers and ensembles; without them the "methods of 2026" claim is dated.
